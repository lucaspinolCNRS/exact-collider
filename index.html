Using this data

Everything this page produces — the shapes, the extracted CSVs, the figures — is free to use, modify and redistribute for any purpose, academic or otherwise; there is nothing to ask for. The raw tables of the companion paper (public domain, CC0), this page and two Python modules that compute the same shapes (MIT licence) are in the repository. If it contributes to work you publish, please cite the two papers it comes from:

  • L. Pinol, New exact bispectrum shapes in multifield inflation, arXiv:2607.15251.
  • L. Pinol, Exact bispectra in strongly mixed multifield inflation (companion paper), arXiv:2609.38167.

The Planck PR4 binned bispectrum shape is not ours: it is the release of O. H. E. Philcox, What Shape is the Inflationary Bispectrum?, arXiv:2603.17004, used under the MIT licence of its release. Cite it too if you use anything derived from the data comparison.

This page is one self-contained file that also runs offline: download it and open it in any browser.

What has been unlocked is remembered by this browser alone. Lock again, at the top of the page, forgets it and puts the gatekeeper back — so the game is worth playing a second time, and worth handing to someone else.

The shapes are the exact tree-level bispectra of the effective theory of inflationary fluctuations with a massive isocurvature scalar σ whose quadratic mixing ρ π̇cσ is resummed to all orders in λ = ρ/H. They are computed here in your browser from the master formulas of the companion paper — the complex-parameter ₂F₁, the leg kernels and the Schwinger-parameter integral are all evaluated live, not interpolated from a table. and λ < 6 are the range over which the numerics are converged. The S/N plane is the companion paper's comparison with Planck, drawn cell by cell from its adaptive scan; only the pointer on it is live, and the star marks the largest excursion of the channel, at the exact lattice point of the scan.
